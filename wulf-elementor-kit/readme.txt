=== Wulf Elementor Kit ===
Requires at least: 6.2
Requires PHP: 7.4
Requires Plugins: elementor
Stable tag: 1.22.0

The Wulf Diamond Jewelers website as Elementor widgets: every section, the site header and footer,
and every page of wulfdiamondjewelers.com as a ready-made template you can pick and build.

* Main: Home, Home (simpler), Home (conversion), Home (daylight), Home (cinematic), Home (brand film),

== Getting started ==

1. Install and activate Elementor, then this plugin.
2. Go to Wulf Kit > Settings and check your phone numbers, address, hours and brand colors.
   These feed every widget, so you only type them once.
3. Go to Wulf Kit > Pages & templates. Tick the pages you want (or "Select all") and click
   "Build selected pages". They're saved as drafts first, so your live site doesn't change.
4. Open any of them with "Edit in Elementor" and change anything you like.
5. When a page is ready, click "Go live" next to it. If your current site already has a page at
   that address, it moves to Pages > Trash (restorable for 30 days) and the new page takes its
   place, so links and Google keep working. For Home, "Go live" makes it the site's homepage.

Rebuilding a page that's already built makes a fresh copy; your edited copy is never overwritten.

The Single Product layout page is the design for every product page: after building it, choose it
in Settings > OM Catalog > Product Page Layout.

The header and footer are saved under Templates > Saved Templates as "Wulf · Header" and
"Wulf · Footer", and appear on every page. Change which templates are used (or turn them off)
in Wulf Kit > Settings. The menu is a normal WordPress menu under Appearance > Menus.

Your own logo picture: in the header (Logo > Logo: Image) and the footer (Logo & about > Logo:
Image), "Size the logo by" Height or Width, then set it for computers, and with the device icon
for tablets and phones. Phones keep a smaller header logo of their own (40px high, or 140px wide)
until you change it there. A logo taller than the header bar makes the bar grow with it; the bar's
own height is in Style > Header bar. The footer logo never gets wider than its column.

The map in the Visit sections: Wulf Kit > Settings > "Map in the Visit sections" shows the live
Google map (the real streets and places around the shop, with your pin; visitors can move and zoom
it), the simple drawn map (an illustration that opens Google Maps) or no map, on every page at once.
"Map zoom" sets how close the live map starts, from 12 (more of the town) to 19 (closest); 16 shows
the streets around the shop. A Visit section can choose its own in Elementor (Showroom > Map). On
phones the Visit section has no map, and "Get directions" opens Google Maps.

== Design sets ==

Wulf Kit > Design sets switches the whole site in one go. A set is a homepage plus the look that
every other page, the header, the footer and the shop follow:

* Night: Home (conversion) as the homepage; the dark header, hero and footer, gold buttons and
  rounded corners. Every other page keeps its own design.
* Daylight: Home (daylight) as the homepage; warm paper and white, ink buttons, square corners, and
  every other page in Daylight too.
* Daylight, cut corners: the same, with the corners of buttons, cards, fields and photos cut away
  like a step-cut diamond.

"Preview on your site" shows a set on the real site, on every page, to you only (you need to be
logged in as an administrator); a small bar at the bottom switches between the sets, makes the one
you're looking at live, or stops the preview. Visitors keep seeing the live set meanwhile. The admin
bar on the site has the same choices under "Design".

Choose a set, then "Make this set live":

* Pages: keep every page as it is (they change to the new look by themselves and your edits stay),
  or also build the pages that aren't built yet and put every library page live, or rebuild every
  page fresh from the library (your current copies go to the trash). A page from your old site at the
  same address goes to the trash when the library page takes its place.
* Your other homepage versions (the homepage being replaced, and any other homepage from the library
  that's published): unpublish them and keep them as drafts (so you can switch back in one click),
  move them to the trash, or leave them as they are.

The set's homepage is built first if it isn't yet, and the Wulf header and footer are set up if they
aren't. Look and Corners in Wulf Kit > Settings change to match the set. After the switch, "Switch
back" makes the previous set live again. Nothing is deleted for good: Pages > Trash keeps pages for
30 days. Page caches from WP Rocket, LiteSpeed, W3 Total Cache, WP Super Cache, SiteGround, WP Fastest
Cache and Breeze are cleared for you; clear any other cache yourself.

== Daylight look and corners ==

Wulf Kit > Settings > Look: Night (the dark header, hero and footer) or Daylight: warm paper and white
instead of night, ink buttons, gold kept for small accents, a hallmark stamp before the hero's label
(Settings > Hallmark stamp, "WULF ◆ 1971" to start with, empty for none) and a fine line in white,
yellow and rose gold under the big headlines. Cards lose their outlines: white on paper sections,
paper on white ones. Sections designed dark (page banners, calls to action, the 4Cs) read as paper:
page banners like the homepage hero, the rest a deeper paper so the backgrounds still alternate. A
section given a dark colour of its own in Style > Section stays dark, with light text. The brand
films and the 4Cs diamond play in a dark display case with a fine edge of white, yellow and rose gold.

Settings > Corners: Rounded, Square, or Cut (the corners cut away like a step-cut diamond) for
buttons, cards, fields and frames. Automatic: rounded at night, square by day. Cut corners go with the
Daylight look; on a Night page they show square. Dots, metal swatches and faces stay round. Square and
cut corners reach the shop too (OM Catalog: catalog, product pages, ring builder, quick view, saved
list): its buttons, fields, chips and cards turn square, and with cut corners its product pictures and
main buttons are cut as well. Rounded corners leave the shop's own design.

One page can choose its own look and corners in Elementor: Page settings > Wulf look. Save, then
reload to see the change.

"Home (daylight)" in the page library is the conversion homepage in Daylight, built around its first
screen: a display case (Hero > Layout > "Display case"). The headline runs across the top, the turning
piece sits large in a white case in the middle with its number (N° 01) and a 360° mark, the words and
buttons are on its left, and the piece's name, metal and price, the other pieces and three short
points are on its right. With square corners the case gets fine gold corner marks; with cut corners,
three step-cut lines ripple out around it like the facets of an emerald cut. The booking bar rests on
the case's bottom edge as a white card (Booking bar > "Rest on the section above"); on phones it shows
one button per topic, and a tap opens the booking window with that topic chosen ("Phones: one tap per
topic"). Then "What brings you in?", the ring style quiz, "Since 1971", how a visit works, the ring
designer, questions and booking, the backgrounds alternating paper and white. Build it next to your
homepage, compare, and "Go live" with the one you like.

"Since 1971" is the page's one dark chapter: The Suitcase beside your story, its years lighting up
under it as it plays, "Watch our story" with sound, the promise "Honest advice, and never a rushed
decision." signed by Cullen, and the only gold button on the page. It is the Brand film widget with
Section > "In the Daylight look": "A dark chapter (stays dark)", and Content > "A promise, in large
italics" and "Signed under the promise". In Daylight the film opens on the suitcase and "1971" rather
than on the line drawing on black (also on About us).

Daylight titles: a small chapter label with the set's mark before it (a square, or an octagon with cut
corners), the title on the left and its intro on the right, and the words you put between *stars*
in gold italics. The three-gold line stays under the hero headline and the story chapter only. With
cut corners, only frames, pictures and the main buttons are cut; options, chips and tabs stay square.

Page banners in Daylight hold their picture in the same case as the homepage piece (corner marks or
step-cut lines). Engagement rings and the shop pages (wedding bands, rings, earrings, necklaces,
pendants, bracelets) show a piece from the line, turning where there's a 360° video (Page banner >
Picture > "Turning video"). Their "Show it in" is "The Daylight look only", so in Night the banners
stay text only, as before.

== Booking panel and conversions ==

Every "Book" button on the site (header, hero, phone bar, tray, Spotlight, ring designer, any button
linked to #visit) opens one short booking panel instead of jumping to a form: what it's about, a day
and time of day, then name and mobile. It shows the piece the visitor was looking at (the Spotlight
ring in its metal, the ring designer's design, the pieces on their tray), and the request email says
which piece and which button it came from. The thank-you screen offers "Add to my calendar" and
directions. Set the title, topics (with what to bring) and thank-you message in Wulf Kit > Settings >
Booking & conversions; untick "Booking panel" to go back to jumping to the visit form.

Links can open it too: add ?book=1 (and &topic=Repair) to any page address, e.g. in an ad or email.

Booking calendar (SimplyBook): with the OM Catalog plugin, the panel and the "Book a visit" form
can show your SimplyBook.me calendar instead of the booking form, or both as two tabs ("Book an
appointment" and "Request a visit"). Choose it in Settings > OM Catalog > Inquiries, on the
"Wulf kit" row; a Visit widget can also choose for itself (Booking form > Show visitors). With
both, a day picked in the booking bar opens the form tab. The calendar loads only when the panel
or form opens or comes into view, and offers the booking page if it can't load. Measured:
booking_calendar_view when the calendar has loaded.

On computers a slim booking bar stays at the bottom of the screen after the first screen (open now,
"Book a free consultation", the phone number). It steps aside near the booking form and the footer,
and a visitor can close it. Turn it off in Settings > "Booking button on computers".

== Ring style quiz ==

Four picture questions (diamond shape, setting, metal, budget), then three rings from the catalog
that match, the top one turning in the chosen gold. Every answer has a "Not sure yet". Matching uses
the shape, the setting and the metal; the budget is passed on with the booking or the email and never
hides a ring (setting prices alone would mislead). When a ring is shown with a different diamond shape
or metal than chosen, a short line says so.

"Book to try these on" opens the booking popup with the three rings (they also go on the visitor's
tray) and the answers in the request email. "Email me my matches" sends the visitor their three rings
with photos, prices, catalog links and a booking button, sends you a copy, and passes the lead to the
CRM when that is on. The email only ever contains the quiz as saved on the page, and one address gets
it at most three times a day.

Edit the questions, shapes, setting styles (photo, short line, drawing), budget choices and the rings
to match in the widget. Rings earlier in the list win a tie, so put favorites first.

"Measure clicks and bookings" sends cta_click, click_to_call, get_directions, booking_open,
booking_step, booking_request, generate_lead, quiz_start, quiz_step, quiz_complete and
quiz_email_open to the Google Analytics, Tag Manager or Meta pixel
already on the site (nothing is added). Mark generate_lead as a key event in GA4 to count bookings as
conversions. Add ?wk_debug_events=1 to a page address to watch them in the browser console.

== Saved pieces: one list ==

The heart on the product row and the Spotlight, the ring designer's save button and the quiz's
"Book to try these on" save pieces for the visitor (kept on their device, no account).

With the OM Catalog plugin (1.46 or later) and its Saved designs on (Settings > OM Catalog > Look &
feel), they all save into the catalog's own list, so there is one list for the whole site: the
homepage, the catalog and product pages. The header's heart button shows the count and opens the
catalog's Saved panel: the designs in the gold colour they were saved in, "Book to see them in
person" (opens the booking window with them), Compare, Share list, Drop a hint and "Email me my
list" (you get a copy under Inquiries and in your CRM when that is on). The catalog's floating Saved
button stays away. Only pieces with a catalog page can be saved there, so a product row piece
linked to another page shows no heart. Pieces already on a visitor's tray from before move into the
list by themselves.

Without OM Catalog, or with its Saved designs off, the kit keeps its own tray: the header's tray
button opens it, with "Book to see them in person" and, when OM Catalog is active, "Email me my
tray" (OM Catalog's saved-designs email, following its "Email me my list" switch). Measured:
generate_lead with form_type tray_email.

Either way, the booking request lists the saved pieces. Turn saving off altogether with Wulf Kit >
Settings > "Saved pieces tray".

== Drop a hint ==

With the OM Catalog plugin 1.39.0 or later, the Spotlight (beside its buttons) and the ring style quiz
results (beside "Start over") show a small "Drop a hint" link. It opens the OM Catalog's hint form with
the ring on screen in the gold showing (the quiz sends its three matches), so a visitor can email it to
a partner with a note and their ring size. The partner's private page has "Book a viewing", which opens
this kit's booking popup with the ring filled in and "From a hint" in the request email.

Each widget has a "Drop a hint" link switch (Spotlight: Rings; quiz: Results). The link text, and
turning the feature off everywhere, are in Settings > OM Catalog > Look & feel > Drop a hint.

== Google reviews ==

The Reviews widget shows reviews you type in, your live Google rating and reviews in the kit's own
design, or the widget of a reviews plugin you already use.

A reviews plugin, on every page at once (for example Widgets for Google Reviews by Trustindex):

1. Set the plugin up as its own instructions say (connect your Google profile, pick a layout).
2. Paste its shortcode, like [trustindex no-registration=google], into Wulf Kit > Settings > Google
   reviews > "Reviews plugin shortcode" and save.

Every Wulf Reviews section on the site then shows that plugin's reviews under its small label,
title and "Read all reviews on Google" link, in place of the kit's rating and review cards. Nothing
on the pages needs editing. The plugin's widget keeps its own look (the kit's styles stay out of
it): choose its layout and colors in the plugin's settings. Pick one of its light styles: the Reviews
sections are light, and a style made for dark backgrounds (white names and text) can hardly be read
on them. The section's background, label, title and link are styled in the Reviews section as usual
(Section and Style tabs). Clear the link text in a Reviews section to hide the kit's link there. If the
plugin is switched off, or shows nothing yet, the sections show the kit's own reviews instead. To
use a plugin on one section only, leave the setting empty and set that section's "Reviews come from"
to "A reviews plugin (shortcode)", with its own shortcode if it needs a different one.

Live Google reviews in the kit's design: set the Reviews widget's "Reviews come from" to "Google,
live" and click Update. This live rating also shows in the hero's trust line and in "Why Wulf".

Free, with Featurable (no Google key or card needed):

1. Sign up free at featurable.com with the Google account that manages your Business Profile.
2. Create a Google reviews widget for the shop.
3. Click Embed, then API, and copy the widget ID.
4. Paste it into Wulf Kit > Settings > Google reviews > "Featurable widget ID" and save. Pasting the
   whole embed code works too. A line above the field says what the site shows now, or what's wrong.

The site asks Featurable for your reviews twice a day ("Refresh every (hours)"), and Featurable
checks Google about every two days. Visitors always get a saved copy, so pages stay fast, and if
Featurable can't be reached the last reviews received keep showing (before the first ones arrive,
the typed-in reviews show). The widget shows reviews with written text and at least the stars you
choose (4 by default), the ones with a few sentences first; very long ones are shortened, and "Read
all reviews on Google" opens them all. Reviews are shown in the reviewer's own words, not Google's
translation. Featurable is a separate service: its free plan and limits are its own, so check
featurable.com if anything changes there.

Or with your own Google key: a Google Places API key and Place ID (needs a Google Cloud billing
account; Google shares up to 5 reviews and picks them). When both are filled in, Featurable is used.

== Business details for Google ==

The homepage tells Google, in a way visitors don't see, that the site belongs to a jewelry store:
its name, address, main phone, opening hours and directions link, all taken from Wulf Kit > Settings
(a "JewelryStore" entry in the page's structured data). Google uses it to match the site with the
shop in search and on Maps. It is on by default. Wulf Kit > Settings > Business details for Google
can turn it off and adds a few optional extras:

* Map pin: latitude and longitude, like 41.41672, -87.36543. In Google Maps, right-click the shop's
  pin: the numbers at the top of the menu copy them.
* Price range: like $$$ or $500 to $25,000.
* Your profiles elsewhere: one link per line (Google Business Profile, Facebook, Instagram, Yelp,
  The Knot and so on).
* Store photo: a link to a photo of the showroom or storefront. Empty uses the showroom photo that
  comes with the kit.

Type the address as street, town, state and ZIP with commas between them (10460 Broadway, Crown
Point, IN 46307) so Google gets each part. "What Google gets" shows the exact entry; once the site is
live, Google's Rich Results Test checks it the way Google reads it. The email for visit requests is
never included. Star ratings are left out on purpose: Google doesn't show stars for reviews a business
publishes about itself. If Yoast Local SEO or Rank Math's Local SEO module already publishes your
business details, the kit leaves its entry out, so Google never gets two versions.

For developers: the `wk_store_schema` filter turns the entry on or off, `wk_store_schema_data`
changes it.

== Cinematic film (3D) ==

"Home (cinematic)" is the conversion homepage told as a short film. It opens on a diamond turning
under the spotlights of a jewelry case; as visitors scroll it comes close, a ring is cast and set
around it, the stone changes shape, the gold changes color and the camera moves in on the hidden
details. The ring is one of ours, modelled on the catalog: the Round 4-Prong Solitaire (85121) as it
builds, and at the end the Oval Hidden Halo Engagement Ring with a diamond band (51162-E), named in a
small caption with a link to it. Nothing is a video or a 3D file: the ring, the diamond and the
studio lights are drawn live in the browser, and the diamond follows the light through every facet,
which is where its sparkle and coloured fire come from.

The film comes in three parts, with the conversion sections in between:

1. The opening (the first screen): the headline, "Book a free consultation" and "Design your ring",
   then the band and prongs build around the stone. The booking bar rests on its last frame.
2. Shapes and metals, before the favorites: round, emerald, pear and oval (the emerald a true
   step cut), then white, yellow and rose gold. Visitors can tap a shape or a metal to jump to it.
3. The details and your story, before "Why Wulf": the hidden halo and the pavé, then "Independent
   since 1971".

It is a separate page, a variation of "Home (conversion)": building it never changes the conversion
page, which keeps its own address and stays exactly as it is. The cinematic version has every
conversion piece of the original: the same booking buttons, reassurance, "Or call" line with the live
open status and trust points on the first screen, then the same booking bar, "What brings you in?",
favorites, why Wulf, ring designer, questions and booking form. It is shorter: the ring style quiz
and "How a visit works" are left out, because the film's shapes and metals do the quiz's job and the
booking bar and form already say how a visit works. Both stay on "Home (conversion)".

To try it: in Wulf Kit > Pages & templates, build "Home (cinematic)", open it, compare it with your
current homepage, and click "Go live" when you like it. "Go live" only chooses which version is the
homepage; the other versions stay published at their own addresses, so you can switch back any time. Every word, button and link is editable in
Elementor (choose a part of the film, then its words); camera moves and timing are built in. Each part
can also be added to any page with the "Wulf · Cinematic film (3D)" widget, once per page, in order.

Built "Home (cinematic)" with version 1.11? The film updates by itself, but the page's sections are
saved in the page: to get the shorter 1.12 layout, build "Home (cinematic)" again with "Build a fresh
copy" (the copy you have is kept, and "Home (conversion)" is not touched).

Speed and devices:

* The 3D only loads on pages that use the film: about 240 KB compressed, after the page has shown.
  Computers that can run the best quality also fetch a small add-on (about 80 KB) for soft contact
  shadows.
* A still picture of each part shows straight away and stays wherever 3D cannot run (very old phones,
  Data Saver); the words and buttons work the same either way.
* Every computer gets the quality it can draw smoothly. While the first still picture shows, the
  film times a small copy of its heaviest moment, then the level it picks (the graphics chip
  matters, not the processor). A weak chip is never handed a frame heavy enough to upset it. That
  level is set for the full-screen close-up; wherever the stone is smaller the film draws sharper. While it plays, it lowers the resolution, then the
  quality, within a second or two if frames slow down.
* The film stays on screen while visitors scroll on any theme. A theme setting or custom CSS that
  hides overflow on a page wrapper (a common fix for sideways scrolling) quietly stops that; the film
  adjusts such wrappers (the same look) or pins itself.
* A computer that can't keep up even on the lightest level, or whose browser or graphics driver
  fails, gets the still pictures instead, with the words still playing as visitors scroll. It never
  sits frozen, and it remembers this for the rest of the visit. Nothing is drawn while the film is
  off screen.
* Visitors who ask their device for less motion (Windows "Animation effects" off, Mac "Reduce
  motion") see each chapter as a still, composed frame.
* To see what the film decided on a computer, add ?filmcheck to the page address: a small box shows
  the kit version, the graphics chip, whether the film stays on screen by itself, the quality level,
  frames a second, or why the 3D is off.
* Speed plugins: exclude assets/js/wulf-film.js from "Delay JavaScript" (WP Rocket, LiteSpeed Cache,
  Perfmatters and similar) and turn off Cloudflare Rocket Loader for it, or the film waits for the
  first tap before it starts.

== Brand films ==

Your two films ("Wulf Diamond Jewelers", 1:15, and "The Suitcase", 0:39) are cut into short silent
parts that come with the kit: highlights (a diamond, four ring styles, fine jewelry), the eight
shapes, ring styles, fine jewelry, the ring builder, custom design (design, wax, cast, set) and
The Suitcase's story since 1971. Each part comes in two copies (a smaller AV1 one for Chrome, Edge,
Firefox and newer Apple devices, and an H.264 one that plays everywhere) with a still picture.

Where they are used:

* Home (brand film): the highlights beside the headline as the first screen, the film in five
  chapters, and The Suitcase beside your story with its years lighting up as it plays.
* About: The Suitcase is the first screen; the custom section plays design, wax, cast and set.
* Custom Jewelry: the four steps as a film right under the banner.
* Diamond Jewelry: the eight shapes before the 4Cs.
* Engagement Rings: the ring builder film after the catalog.

Pages you already built keep their design: build a fresh copy in Wulf Kit > Pages & templates to
get the film sections, compare, then "Go live".

The full films with sound play from your Media Library, so the plugin stays light: upload the two
MP4 files once (Media > Add New), then choose them in Wulf Kit > Settings > Brand films. Until a film
is chosen its "Watch the film" buttons stay hidden (the Elementor editor shows a note where they
go). Any section can also use your own video instead, under Film > "My own video" and Watch the film
> "My own video".

How they behave: a film only downloads when its section nears the screen, plays silently while on
screen and rests when scrolled away; the first screen's film waits until the page has loaded. A
small button pauses it. Visitors whose device asks for less motion, or who save mobile data, see
the still picture with a play button instead. The films' black melts into the section's dark
background, so keep film sections dark (Style > Section > Background).

Measured (with "Measure clicks and bookings" on): film_play (the window opened), film_complete,
film_moment (a moment under a film chosen) and film_chapter (a chapter tab chosen).

== Visit requests and newsletter ==

The "Book a visit" form and the footer sign-up email you (Wulf Kit > Settings > "Visit requests
go to"; empty uses the site admin email). If the OM Catalog plugin is active and "Send to your CRM"
is ticked, they are also passed to its lead sending (GoHighLevel / webhook).
